## Summary: Integration of Financial Statement Analysis Techniques (CFA Level II — Financial Statement Analysis)

---

### Phase 5: Develop and Communicate Conclusions and Recommendations

Having worked through DuPont decomposition, asset/capital structure analysis, segment and product-group capital allocation, accruals/earnings quality, cash flow adequacy, and a sum-of-the-parts valuation, the analyst organizes his findings into a report for the portfolio manager, split into two classes.

**Support for an investment in Nestlé shares:**
- Nestlé has the **financial stability** to fund both organic growth and its growth-by-acquisition strategy — liquidity and cash flow are more than adequate, leverage is low, and the capital structure can support future operations and strategy.
- **Operating cash flow has consistently exceeded operating earnings**, and the ratio has been favorable; measures of cash flow versus reinvestment, debt, and debt-servicing capacity all indicate financial strength.
- Decomposing earnings/valuation into Nestlé-only vs. L'Oréal shows the **implied Nestlé-only P/E is lower** than both L'Oréal's and the overall market's — given Nestlé's demonstrated cash flow and low leverage, this looks like a potential **valuation opportunity**.

**Causes for concern:**
- Despite world-class brands and global reach, **core (Nestlé-only) profitability has deteriorated** every year, as shown by the ROE decomposition — and the decline persists even after adjusting for unusual charges.
- **Cash returns on total assets have declined every year** since 2012, corroborating the DuPont-based finding with an independent, cash-based measure.
- Acquisitions in the **Nutrition and Health Science** product group do not appear to build on the company's traditional strengths and have not remedied the core-profitability decline.
- **Capital allocation priorities are questionable**: the segments/product groups with the strongest EBIT margins (Nestlé Nutrition, Nutrition and Health Science) rank very low in *return on assets* — raising the question of whether management is **overpaying for acquisitions**.
- The **2014 goodwill write-down** on earlier acquisitions, occurring while new acquisitions continue, signals potentially ineffective capital allocation.

**Analyst's conclusion**: Nestlé is **not clearly a good investment opportunity at this time**; the analyst recommends **waiting** to see whether a further valuation discount emerges or operating performance improves.

> **Key insight**: Notice that the report is structured as a **balanced two-sided case** (support vs. concern), directly answering the specific questions the portfolio manager posed in Phase 1 — this is what "Phase 5 output" should look like on the exam: a report that closes the loop back to the stated purpose, not a generic list of ratios.

### Phase 6: Follow-up

The portfolio manager, despite the analyst's cautious recommendation, believed Nestlé's issues were **temporary** and the valuation discount was attractive enough to proceed — but only with a **cautious, less-than-core** position, smaller than originally planned, specifically **because of** the analyst's capital-allocation concerns. She directs the analyst to:
- **Continually re-evaluate** the holding;
- Treat **unproductive capital spending as a trigger** for eliminating the position;
- **Update the research report at each reporting period**, with particular emphasis on the **accruals tests**, **cash flow support of earnings**, and **return on assets**.

> **Key insight**: Phase 6 is not a one-time sign-off — it is an explicit **commitment to repeat Phases 2–5 periodically**, with the analyst told in advance which specific metrics (accruals ratio, cash-based earnings support, ROA) should drive a *future* sell decision. This is the exam's model answer for what "follow-up" concretely means.

### The Six-Phase Framework — Exam Checklist

| # | Phase | What happens | Typical outputs |
|---|-------|---------------|------------------|
| 1 | **Define purpose and context** | Clarify *why* the analysis is being done (equity investment? credit rating? leverage assessment?) and what questions must be answered | Purpose statement; list of questions; report format/timetable/budget |
| 2 | **Collect input data** | Gather financial statements, industry/economic data, management discussions, site visits | Organized financial statements and data tables |
| 3 | **Process data** | Convert raw data into analytically useful form | Adjusted/common-size statements, ratios, graphs, forecasts |
| 4 | **Analyze/interpret** | Draw conclusions from the processed data (often merged with Phase 3 in practice) | Analytical results |
| 5 | **Communicate conclusions/recommendations** | Write the report; make the recommendation | Analysis report answering the Phase 1 questions; investment/credit recommendation |
| 6 | **Follow-up** | Repeat the cycle periodically | Updated reports and recommendations; explicit re-evaluation triggers |

### Formula Summary

**DuPont decomposition:**

$$\boxed{ROE = \text{Net profit margin} \times \text{Asset turnover} \times \text{Financial leverage}}$$

$$\boxed{ROE = \text{Tax burden} \times \text{Interest burden} \times \text{EBIT margin} \times \text{Asset turnover} \times \text{Financial leverage}}$$

| Component | Formula |
|-----------|---------|
| Tax burden | Net income / EBT |
| Interest burden | EBT / EBIT |
| EBIT margin | EBIT / Sales |
| Asset turnover | Sales / Avg. total assets |
| Financial leverage | Avg. total assets / Avg. total equity |

**Accruals ratios:**

$$\boxed{\text{Balance-sheet-based accruals ratio}_t = \frac{NOA_t - NOA_{t-1}}{(NOA_t + NOA_{t-1})/2}}$$

$$\boxed{\text{Cash-flow-based accruals ratio}_t = \frac{NI_t - (CFO_t + CFI_t)}{(NOA_t + NOA_{t-1})/2}}$$

**Cash flow adequacy and return:**

$$\boxed{\text{Cash ROA} = \frac{\text{Cash generated from operations}}{\text{Average total assets}}}$$

$$\boxed{\text{Cash flow to reinvestment} = \frac{\text{Cash from operations}}{\text{Capex} + \text{Intangible asset expenditure}}}$$

$$\boxed{\text{Cash flow to total debt} = \frac{\text{Cash from operations}}{\text{Total debt}}}$$

$$\boxed{\text{Cash flow interest coverage} = \frac{\text{Cash from operations}}{\text{Cash interest paid}}}$$

**Capital allocation and valuation:**

$$\boxed{\text{Capital allocation ratio} = \frac{\text{Segment \% of total capex}}{\text{Segment \% of total assets}}}$$

$$\boxed{\text{Segment cash ROA} = \frac{\text{Segment EBIT} + \text{Segment D\&A}}{\text{Average segment total assets}}}$$

$$\boxed{\text{Cash conversion cycle} = DSO + DOH - \text{Days payables}}$$

> Sum-of-the-parts P/E: strip a separately-traded associate's market value and attributable earnings out of consolidated figures to compute an implied P/E for the "core" business.

### Question Set Answers — Bickchip Enterprises Practice Problem

**Scenario**: Quentin Abay, CFA, an analyst for a private equity firm, is evaluating **Bickchip Enterprises** for acquisition, using the same DuPont/segment/accruals toolkit as the Nestlé case study.

**Selected financial data ($ thousands):**

| | 2020 | 2019 | 2018 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 72,448 | 66,487 | 55,781 |
| EBIT | 6,270 | 4,710 | 3,609 |
| EBT | 5,101 | 4,114 | 3,168 |
| Net income | 4,038 | 3,345 | 2,576 |
| Asset turnover | 0.79 | 0.76 | 0.68 |
| Assets/Equity (leverage) | 3.09 | 3.38 | 3.43 |

**Segment data ($ thousands):**

| Segment | Capital Employed 2020 | Capex 2020 | 2020 EBIT Margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Automation Equipment | 10,705 | 700 | 11% |
| Power and Industrial | 15,805 | 900 | 5% |
| Medical Equipment | 22,870 | 908 | 8% |

**Earnings quality data ($ thousands):** Net income 4,038 (2020); CFO 9,822; CFI (10,068); Average NOA 43,192; cash taxes paid 1,930.

---

**Q1. Over 2018–2020, Bickchip's ROE is best described as:**
**Answer: Trending higher.** ROE = NPM × AT × Leverage: 2018 = (2,576/55,781) × 0.68 × 3.43 = 4.6% × 0.68 × 3.43 = **10.8%**; 2019 ≈ **12.9%**; 2020 ≈ **13.6%**.

**Q2. Based on the DuPont analysis, Abay's belief that ROE could improve without operational changes is most likely based on:**
**Answer: Leverage.** Profit margins and asset turnover both *improved* over the period, but financial leverage *declined* (3.43 → 3.38 → 3.09), partially offsetting the operational gains — meaning ROE would have been even higher had leverage not fallen. (A firm can raise ROE via leverage without any operational improvement — the concern being tested.)

**Q3. The business segment best suited for divestiture, using capital-employed returns, is:**
**Answer: Power and Industrial.** It has the lowest EBIT margin (5%) yet consumes about 31–36% of total capital employed/capex — and its share of capital expenditures has been *rising* (32% → 36% over three years) even as its margin lags — the opposite of a rational allocation.

**Q4. Bickchip's cash-flow-based accruals ratio in 2020 is closest to:**
**Answer: 9.9%.** $[NI - (CFO + CFI)] / \text{Avg. NOA} = [4,038 - (9,822 - 10,068)]/43,192 = [4,038-(-246)]/43,192 = 4,284/43,192 = 9.9\%$.

**Q5. The cash-flow-based accruals ratio trend from 2018 to 2020 indicates:**
**Answer: A modest net improvement in earnings quality** (the ratio falls from 2018, dips further in 2019, then partially rises back in 2020, but the net three-year change is a decline).

**Q6. The ratio of operating cash flow before interest and taxes to operating income (EBIT) for 2020 is closest to:**
**Answer: 1.9.** Since Bickchip (IFRS) classifies interest expense as a financing cash flow, only the tax add-back is needed: $(9,822 + 1,930)/6,270 = 11,752/6,270 = 1.9$.

**Q7. Based on this ratio's trend, Abay should conclude:**
**Answer: Bickchip's earnings are backed by cash flow.** The ratio rises steadily from roughly 1.2 to 1.9 over the period — consistently above 1.0 and trending up, indicating operating earnings are well supported by cash generation.

### Exam Tips

- **Know the six phases cold, in order, with what each produces**:
  1. Define purpose and context → purpose statement, key questions, report format/timetable
  2. Collect input data → organized financial statements/data
  3. Process data → adjusted/common-size statements, ratios
  4. Analyze/interpret → analytical conclusions (often merged with Phase 3)
  5. Communicate conclusions/recommendations → the analysis report and recommendation, explicitly answering the Phase 1 questions
  6. Follow-up → periodic repetition of the cycle, with pre-specified triggers for revisiting the recommendation
- **DuPont is a starting point, not a mandate** — the exam may test other valid entry points (e.g., time-series common-size income statement); what matters is executing the toolkit correctly once a technique is chosen.
- When a large **equity-method associate** exists, expect to adjust net income (subtract associate income) and total assets (subtract investment in associate) for margin/turnover, while typically **leaving leverage unadjusted**.
- **Reported (consolidated) trends can diverge sharply from core/adjusted trends** — a rising reported ROE can mask a declining core ROE if driven by one-off gains (equity-method disposals, revaluations). This divergence is the single most tested pattern in this LM.
- **Segment/product-group EBIT margin rankings can diverge from cash-return-on-assets rankings** — always test capital allocation decisions against a cash-based measure, not accrual margins alone.
- **Accruals ratios**: judge by trend (rising = warning), not absolute level; **cash-flow-based ratio = [NI − (CFO+CFI)]/Avg. NOA**; **balance-sheet-based ratio = ΔNOA/Avg. NOA**.
- **Cash flow adequacy** (to reinvestment, to total debt, interest coverage) tests financial *capacity*; it can be strong even while capital *allocation* quality is weak — these are separate questions.
- **Sum-of-the-parts (implied) P/E**: strip a separately-traded associate's market value and attributable earnings from the consolidated numbers to reveal the multiple the market assigns to core operations — the two multiples can diverge meaningfully.
- **Phase 5 output should directly answer the Phase 1 questions**, organized as a balanced case (supporting points vs. concerns), ending in a clear recommendation.
- **Phase 6 is a forward commitment**, not a formality — a real follow-up plan names the specific metrics (e.g., accruals ratio, cash-flow support of earnings, ROA) that will trigger a future change in the recommendation.
