## Summary: Earnings Quality and Valuation Analysis (CFA Level II — Financial Statement Analysis)

---

### Accruals and Earnings Quality

Having found weak spots in segment capital allocation, the analyst asks a pointed question: **could the company be masking weak operating performance through accounting accruals?** He applies the accruals-ratio toolkit from the earnings-quality module (LM5) to Nestlé.

$$\boxed{\text{Balance-sheet-based accruals ratio}_t = \frac{NOA_t - NOA_{t-1}}{(NOA_t + NOA_{t-1})/2}}$$

$$\boxed{\text{Cash-flow-based accruals ratio}_t = \frac{NI_t - (CFO_t + CFI_t)}{(NOA_t + NOA_{t-1})/2}}$$

where $NOA$ = net operating assets (operating assets − operating liabilities), $NI$ = net income, $CFO$ = cash flow from operations, $CFI$ = cash flow from investing.

**Nestlé accruals ratios (CHF millions):**

| | 2014 | 2013 | 2012 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Balance-sheet-based aggregate accruals (Δ NOA) | 5,380 | (1,955) | 8,114 |
| Average NOA | 81,519 | 79,807 | 76,727 |
| **Balance-sheet-based accruals ratio** | **6.6%** | **−2.4%** | **10.6%** |
| Cash-flow-based aggregate accruals | 3,276 | (2,941) | 9,500 |
| Average NOA | 81,519 | 79,807 | 76,727 |
| **Cash-flow-based accruals ratio** | **4.0%** | **−3.7%** | **12.4%** |

> **Key insight**: Both ratios are **small in absolute magnitude** relative to net operating assets, and — critically — **neither is trending consistently higher** (both actually *decreased* from 2012 to the more recent two years). High *and* rising accruals ratios would be the red flag; this pattern is reassuring and lets the analyst conclude that **accruals are not a major driver of Nestlé's reported results**, clearing the way to look directly at cash flow quality instead.

> **Exam-critical takeaway**: The absolute level of the accruals ratio matters less than the **trend**. A stable or declining accruals ratio, even if not zero, generally signals earnings quality is not deteriorating. A ratio that is high *and* rising is the pattern that should trigger real concern about aggressive accrual accounting.

### Cash Flow Relationships

Next, the analyst directly tests whether **operating earnings are backed by cash**.

**1. Cash generated from operations vs. operating profit (EBIT):**

$$\boxed{\text{Cash conversion ratio} = \frac{\text{Cash generated from operations}}{\text{Operating profit (EBIT)}}}$$

| (CHF millions) | 2014 | 2013 | 2012 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash generated from operations | 17,199 | 18,206 | 18,525 |
| Operating profit (EBIT) | 10,905 | 13,068 | 13,388 |
| **Ratio** | **1.58** | **1.39** | **1.38** |

Cash generated from operations **substantially exceeded** operating profit in every year (ratio well above 1.0) — a reassuring sign of earnings quality.

**2. Cash return on total assets:**

$$\boxed{\text{Cash ROA} = \frac{\text{Cash generated from operations}}{\text{Average total assets}}}$$

| (CHF millions) | 2014 | 2013 | 2012 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash generated from operations | 17,199 | 18,206 | 18,525 |
| Average total assets | 126,946 | 123,160 | 119,659 |
| **Cash return on total assets** | **13.5%** | **14.8%** | **15.5%** |

> **Key insight**: Although the 13.5% cash return in 2014 is still a high absolute number, the **declining trend (15.5% → 14.8% → 13.5%)** echoes the earlier findings — weakening organic profitability and questionable acquisition returns (the Nutrition/Health Science product group, the 2014 goodwill impairment) are showing up in overall asset productivity, not just in the DuPont decomposition.

**3. Cash flow adequacy — reinvestment, debt, and interest coverage:**

$$\boxed{\text{Cash flow to reinvestment} = \frac{\text{Cash from operations}}{\text{Capex} + \text{Intangible asset expenditure}}}$$

$$\boxed{\text{Cash flow to total debt} = \frac{\text{Cash from operations}}{\text{Total debt (current + long-term financial debt + derivative liabilities)}}}$$

$$\boxed{\text{Cash flow interest coverage} = \frac{\text{Cash from operations}}{\text{Cash interest paid}}}$$

| (CHF millions) | 2014 | 2013 | 2012 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash from operations | 17,199 | 18,206 | 18,525 |
| Total reinvestment spending (capex + intangibles) | 4,423 | 5,330 | 5,598 |
| **Cash flow to reinvestment** | **3.89×** | **3.42×** | **3.31×** |
| Total debt | 21,963 | 22,124 | 27,839 |
| **Cash flow to total debt** | **78.3%** | **82.3%** | **66.5%** |
| Cash interest paid | 518 | 505 | 559 |
| **Cash flow interest coverage** | **33.2×** | **36.1×** | **33.1×** |

> **Key insight**: All three ratios indicate **ample financial capacity**: reinvestment needs are covered ~3.3×–3.9× (and the trend is *improving*, unlike the cash-ROA trend above); operating cash flow covers over three-quarters of total debt (implying debt could be repaid in roughly two years even while maintaining current reinvestment); and interest is covered more than 30× by cash flow. Together, this confirms the balance sheet has **ample capacity to fund the growth-by-acquisition strategy** — even though the *quality* of recent acquisitions is in question (files 1–2).

### Decomposition and Analysis of the Company's Valuation

With operating performance, capital structure, and earnings quality assessed, the analyst turns to the **market's valuation** of Nestlé — using a sum-of-the-parts approach to isolate the value the market assigns to "Nestlé-only" operations from the value of the L'Oréal stake (equity-method associate).

**Step 1 — value of the L'Oréal holding (31 Dec 2014):**

| | Amount |
|---|---|
| L'Oréal share price (EUR) | 139.30 |
| Shares held by Nestlé (millions) | 129.881 |
| L'Oréal holding value (EUR millions) | 18,092 |
| CHF/EUR exchange rate | 1.202 |
| **L'Oréal holding value (CHF millions)** | **21,747** |

**Step 2 — Nestlé market value, with and without L'Oréal:**

| | CHF millions |
|---|---|
| Nestlé share price | 72.95 |
| Shares outstanding (millions) | 3,168.4 |
| **Nestlé market capitalization** | **231,135** |
| Less: value of L'Oréal holding | (21,747) |
| **Implied value of Nestlé-only operations** | **209,388** |

L'Oréal represents **9.4%** of Nestlé's total market value; implied Nestlé-only operations represent **90.6%**.

**Step 3 — decompose earnings the same way:**

| | CHF millions |
|---|---|
| Nestlé consolidated earnings (net income) | 14,904 |
| Less: L'Oréal earnings (disclosed contribution) | (934) |
| Nestlé standalone earnings | 13,970 |
| Less: non-controlling interests | (448) |
| **Nestlé standalone earnings to shareholders** | **13,522** |

**Step 4 — compare market value, earnings, and implied P/E:**

| | Market Value | Earnings (attributable) | P/E |
|---|---|---|---|
| L'Oréal | 21,747 | 934 | **23.3×** |
| Implied Nestlé-only | 209,388 | 13,522 | **15.5×** |
| Total Nestlé (as reported) | 231,135 | 14,456 | **16.0×** |

> **Key insight — the punchline**: At the time of analysis (early 2015), Nestlé traded at **16.0× trailing earnings**, a 20% discount to the S&P 500's 19.9×. But once L'Oréal is stripped out, the **implied Nestlé-only P/E is 15.5×** — a *steeper* 22% discount to the market multiple. This is a genuinely useful, non-obvious finding: the "Nestlé-only" business — the part with declining core profitability the analyst already flagged — trades at an even bigger discount than the headline number suggests, while the L'Oréal stake alone (23.3×) trades at a premium to the whole. The market appears to be pricing in some of the same operational skepticism the analyst's own DuPont and segment work uncovered.

### Question Set Answers

**Q1. Both the balance-sheet-based and cash-flow-based accruals ratios for a company are low in absolute value but have risen steadily for four straight years. Should the analyst be more or less concerned than if the ratios were flat at a moderate level?**
A. More concerned. It's the *combination* of high **and rising** that signals earnings-quality deterioration — a low but steadily rising ratio is an early warning sign, even if the current absolute level looks unremarkable.

**Q2. A company's cash-flow-to-total-debt ratio is 78%, and its cash-flow interest coverage is 33×. What can the analyst reasonably conclude?**
A. The company has ample unused debt capacity and financial flexibility — it could take on additional borrowing to fund an attractive investment opportunity (e.g., an acquisition) without straining its ability to service existing obligations.

**Q3. Why does the analyst compute an implied Nestlé-only P/E rather than relying on the consolidated, as-reported P/E?**
A. Because a meaningful chunk of consolidated earnings and market value (here, ~6.5% of earnings and ~9.4% of market cap) comes from a separately-traded associate (L'Oréal) with its *own* market-determined valuation, which can differ materially from its embedded valuation inside Nestlé. Removing it isolates the multiple the market is actually assigning to Nestlé's core, controlled operations — the part most relevant to assessing operational performance.

### Exam Tips (this file)

- **Cash-flow-based accruals ratio = [NI − (CFO + CFI)] / Average NOA**; **balance-sheet-based = ΔNOA / Average NOA**. Judge them on **trend**, not just level — high and rising is the red flag.
- **Cash generated from operations should exceed operating profit (EBIT) consistently** (ratio > 1.0) as a basic earnings-quality check; a ratio persistently below 1.0, or falling toward it, calls earnings quality into question.
- **Cash return on total assets** can trend down even while absolute levels look healthy — always compare to the multi-year trend, not a single year in isolation.
- **Cash flow adequacy ratios** (to reinvestment, to total debt, interest coverage) test whether operating cash flow can fund growth *and* service obligations — strength here can coexist with weakness in *where* that capital is being deployed (segment/product-group findings).
- **Sum-of-the-parts valuation**: when a company holds a significant, separately-traded equity-method investment, strip out its market value and attributable earnings to compute an implied P/E for the "core" business — the two multiples can diverge meaningfully and reveal where market skepticism (or optimism) is actually concentrated.
