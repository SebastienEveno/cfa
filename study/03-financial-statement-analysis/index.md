# Financial Statement Analysis — Module Overview

Intercorporate investments, employee compensation, multinational operations, financial institution analysis, evaluating the quality of financial reports, and an integrated case study applying the full analyst toolkit.

---

## Module 1: Intercorporate Investments

How the degree of influence or control (fair value vs. equity method vs. acquisition method/consolidation) determines the accounting for cross-company holdings under IFRS and US GAAP, and how the same underlying economic stake produces materially different leverage, profitability, and turnover ratios depending solely on which method applies.

- **[Investment Categories and Fair Value Accounting (IFRS 9)](/cfa/study/03-financial-statement-analysis/01-intercorporate-investments/01-investment-categories-and-fair-value/)** — The four investment categories, influence thresholds, and IFRS 9 classification
- **[Equity Method Basics](/cfa/study/03-financial-statement-analysis/01-intercorporate-investments/02-equity-method-basics/)** — Significant influence, excess purchase price amortization, fair value option, and impairment
- **[Equity Method — Transactions with Associates and Disclosure](/cfa/study/03-financial-statement-analysis/01-intercorporate-investments/03-equity-method-transactions-and-disclosure/)** — Upstream/downstream transactions and analytical pitfalls
- **[The Acquisition Method and Consolidation](/cfa/study/03-financial-statement-analysis/01-intercorporate-investments/04-acquisition-method-and-consolidation/)** — Purchase price allocation, goodwill, and non-controlling interests
- **[Financial Statement Presentation, VIEs, and Comparability Issues](/cfa/study/03-financial-statement-analysis/01-intercorporate-investments/05-vies-and-comparability-issues/)** — VIE/SPE consolidation, securitization, and comparability issues
- **[Formula Summary](/cfa/study/03-financial-statement-analysis/01-intercorporate-investments/06-formula-summary/)** — All intercorporate investments formulas in one place

## Module 2: Employee Compensation

How share-based compensation (restricted stock/RSUs and stock options) and post-employment benefit plans (defined contribution and defined benefit) flow through the financial statements, including tax and diluted-share-count effects, disclosures, and the financial-modeling adjustments analysts make.

- **[Types of Employee Compensation and Share-Based Basics](/cfa/study/03-financial-statement-analysis/02-employee-compensation/01-types-of-compensation-and-share-based-basics/)** — Compensation categories and the grant/vesting/settlement timeline
- **[Restricted Stock and Stock Options](/cfa/study/03-financial-statement-analysis/02-employee-compensation/02-restricted-stock-and-stock-options/)** — Accounting mechanics and grant-date fair value measurement
- **[SBC Tax, Share-Count, and Modeling Effects](/cfa/study/03-financial-statement-analysis/02-employee-compensation/03-share-based-comp-effects-and-modeling/)** — Tax windfalls/shortfalls, the treasury stock method, and forecasting shares outstanding
- **[Post-Employment Benefits: DC and DB Plans](/cfa/study/03-financial-statement-analysis/02-employee-compensation/04-post-employment-benefits-db-dc-plans/)** — Funded status, US GAAP/IFRS differences, and disclosures
- **[Formula Summary](/cfa/study/03-financial-statement-analysis/02-employee-compensation/05-formula-summary/)** — All employee compensation formulas in one place

## Module 4: Analysis of Financial Institutions

Why financial-institution analysis diverges from standard equity/credit analysis — leverage as a structural feature rather than a red flag, and regulatory capital adequacy substituting for traditional solvency measures — through the CAMELS framework for banks and the parallel toolkit for property & casualty and life & health insurers.

- **[What Makes Financial Institutions Different](/cfa/study/03-financial-statement-analysis/04-analysis-of-financial-institutions/01-what-makes-financial-institutions-different/)** — Balance sheet structure and global/jurisdictional regulation
- **[CAMELS: Capital Adequacy and Asset Quality](/cfa/study/03-financial-statement-analysis/04-analysis-of-financial-institutions/02-camels-capital-and-asset-quality/)** — Regulatory capital ratios and loan-loss measures
- **[CAMELS: Management, Earnings, Liquidity, Sensitivity](/cfa/study/03-financial-statement-analysis/04-analysis-of-financial-institutions/03-camels-management-earnings-liquidity-sensitivity/)** — The remaining four CAMELS components and the overall assessment
- **[Non-CAMELS Factors and a Worked Bank Example](/cfa/study/03-financial-statement-analysis/04-analysis-of-financial-institutions/04-non-camels-factors-and-worked-example/)** — What CAMELS misses, plus a full worked CAMELS case
- **[Insurance Company Analysis](/cfa/study/03-financial-statement-analysis/04-analysis-of-financial-institutions/05-insurance-company-analysis/)** — Property & casualty and life & health insurer analysis
- **[Formula Summary](/cfa/study/03-financial-statement-analysis/04-analysis-of-financial-institutions/06-formula-summary/)** — All financial institution analysis formulas in one place

## Module 5: Evaluating Quality of Financial Reports

Placing a company's financial reports on a quality spectrum from GAAP-compliant/sustainable down to outright fraud, using quantitative screens (the Beneish M-score, the Altman Z-score) and qualitative red flags drawn from real fraud cases to evaluate earnings, cash flow, and balance sheet quality.

- **[Conceptual Framework and Reporting Problems](/cfa/study/03-financial-statement-analysis/05-evaluating-quality-of-financial-reports/01-conceptual-framework-and-reporting-problems/)** — The quality-of-reporting spectrum, and recognition/classification problems
- **[The Beneish Model and Quantitative Tools](/cfa/study/03-financial-statement-analysis/05-evaluating-quality-of-financial-reports/02-beneish-model-and-quantitative-tools/)** — The full Beneish M-score and other misreporting-detection models
- **[Earnings Quality Indicators](/cfa/study/03-financial-statement-analysis/05-evaluating-quality-of-financial-reports/03-earnings-quality-indicators/)** — Earnings persistence, accruals, mean reversion, and beating benchmarks
- **[Earnings Quality Case Studies](/cfa/study/03-financial-statement-analysis/05-evaluating-quality-of-financial-reports/04-earnings-quality-case-studies/)** — Sunbeam, MicroStrategy, and WorldCom
- **[Bankruptcy Prediction Models](/cfa/study/03-financial-statement-analysis/05-evaluating-quality-of-financial-reports/05-bankruptcy-prediction-models/)** — The Altman Z-score and modern developments
- **[Cash Flow and Balance Sheet Quality](/cfa/study/03-financial-statement-analysis/05-evaluating-quality-of-financial-reports/06-cash-flow-and-balance-sheet-quality/)** — Indicators and evaluation of cash flow and balance sheet quality
- **[Formula Summary](/cfa/study/03-financial-statement-analysis/05-evaluating-quality-of-financial-reports/07-formula-summary/)** — Sources of risk information, all formulas, and exam tips

- **[Earnings Quality and Valuation Analysis](/cfa/study/03-financial-statement-analysis/06-integration-of-fsa-techniques/03-earnings-quality-and-valuation-analysis/)** — Accruals, cash flow relationships, and sum-of-the-parts valuation
