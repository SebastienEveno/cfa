## Summary: Factor Models in Strategic Portfolio Decisions (CFA Level II — Portfolio Management)

---

### Core Insight — Beyond CAPM's One-Size-Fits-All Approach

**CAPM prescription**: All investors should hold just **two funds**:
1. The market portfolio (cap-weighted)
2. The risk-free asset

...varying only the **weighting** between them based on risk tolerance.

**Multifactor model insight**: This is **too simplistic** — different investors have different **comparative advantages and disadvantages** in bearing specific types of risk, which multifactor models can help identify and exploit.

---

### The Key Strategic Questions

$$\boxed{\text{Relative to other investors:}}$$
$$\text{1. What risks do I have a COMPARATIVE ADVANTAGE bearing?}$$
$$\text{2. What risks do I have a COMPARATIVE DISADVANTAGE bearing?}$$

> **Strategic implication**: Investors should **tilt** their strategic asset allocation toward risks where they have an advantage (capturing associated risk premiums) and **away from** risks where they're disadvantaged.

---

### Institutional Example — University Endowments

**Comparative ADVANTAGE**: 
- **Business cycle risk** (equities) — long horizon allows riding out cyclical downturns
- **Liquidity risk** (private equity) — no near-term liquidity needs, can lock up capital for years

**Strategic implication**: Endowments can **tilt allocation toward** these risk factors to capture associated risk premiums that other, more liquidity-constrained investors cannot access as easily.

**Comparative DISADVANTAGE**:
- **Inflation risk** — university costs (faculty salaries, research, facilities) have historically risen **faster than average inflation**

**Strategic implication**: Endowments should be **cautious about excessive inflation risk exposure**, since their liabilities are already disproportionately exposed to above-average cost inflation.

---

### Individual Investor Example — Human Capital Considerations

**Investor A**: Depends on **salary/self-employment income**
- **Highly sensitive to business cycle risk** — job loss risk correlates with recessions
- Comparative **disadvantage** in bearing business cycle risk
- Given two stocks with the **same CAPM beta**, this investor would demand a **higher premium** for procyclical (business-cycle-sensitive) assets

**Investor B**: Has **independent wealth**, no job-loss concerns
- Comparative **advantage** in bearing business cycle risk
- Optimal portfolio would likely **tilt toward greater-than-average exposure** to the business cycle factor, all else equal

$$\boxed{\text{Same CAPM beta} \neq \text{Same optimal holding for every investor} \implies \text{Human capital and other risk exposures matter}}$$

---

### Why This Matters — Beyond CAPM's Simplification

**CAPM's implicit assumption**: All investors care only about **market risk (beta)** — differences in optimal portfolios come **only** from risk tolerance (how much market exposure to hold).

**Multifactor reality**: Investors differ in their **exposure to MULTIPLE risk dimensions** through:
- Human capital (labor income sensitivity to business cycle)
- Investment horizon (ability to bear liquidity/business cycle risk)
- Liability structure (inflation sensitivity of future spending needs)
- Other idiosyncratic circumstances

> **Practical implication**: Two investors with identical CAPM betas may have **very different optimal portfolios** once their full risk profile (across multiple factors) is considered.

---

### Diversification Benefits

**Multifactor models provide better explanatory power** than single-factor (CAPM) models:

$$\boxed{\text{Portfolio characteristics explained by SMB + HML + WML + Market} > \text{Market factor alone}}$$

> This richer factor set allows investors to **better understand and control** their true risk exposures — leading to **more efficient** portfolio construction than relying on a single market beta.

---

### Key Takeaway

$$\boxed{\text{Multifactor models} \implies \text{Richer decision framework than CAPM for strategic asset allocation}}$$

**Core principle**: Investors should:
1. **Identify** which systematic risks they face (via a sound multifactor model)
2. **Analyze** the extent of their exposure to each risk
3. **Tilt** strategic allocation toward risks where they have a **comparative advantage** (to earn associated risk premiums)
4. **Avoid or hedge** risks where they have a **comparative disadvantage**

---

### Exam Tips

- **CAPM prescribes only TWO funds** (market portfolio + risk-free asset) — multifactor models offer a **richer, more nuanced** framework
- **Comparative advantage/disadvantage in bearing risk** is investor-specific — depends on horizon, liquidity needs, human capital, and liability structure
- **Long-horizon investors** (endowments, pension funds) typically have comparative **advantage** in bearing business cycle and liquidity risk
- **Endowments have comparative DISADVANTAGE in inflation risk** — because their spending needs (education costs) tend to rise faster than general inflation
- **Human capital matters**: salaried/self-employed individuals are already exposed to business cycle risk through their JOB — should be cautious about ADDING more procyclical asset exposure
- **Same CAPM beta ≠ same optimal holding** for different investors once multiple risk dimensions are considered
- **Multifactor models (SMB, HML, WML + market) explain portfolio characteristics better** than market factor alone — supports more efficient diversification
- This section ties together the whole Module 4 narrative: multifactor models aren't just for **analysis** (attribution, risk decomposition) — they're also a tool for **making better strategic investment decisions** tailored to an investor's unique risk profile
