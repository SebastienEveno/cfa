## Summary: 1.03 | Expectations and Asset Values (CFA Level II — Economics and Investment Markets)

---

### Learning Outcome
Explain the role of expectations and *changes* in expectations in market valuation.

---

### Two Core Observations from Equation 1

$$\boxed{
\begin{array}{l}
1.\ \text{Asset values depend on \textbf{expected future} cash flows, NOT past cash flows} \\
2.\ \text{Expectations are \textbf{conditional on current information} (time subscript } t\text{)}
\end{array}
}$$

**Implication of #2**: Any information that improves the accuracy/precision of forecasts about future cash flows is **relevant** to valuation — this is why the model is inherently forward-looking and information-sensitive.

---

### News vs. Anticipated Information — The Key Distinction

$$\boxed{\text{News} = \text{Surprise relative to fully anticipated information}}$$

| Type of Information | Effect on Asset Values |
|---|---|
| **Anticipated information** | Already **priced in** — no additional price reaction when it's confirmed |
| **"News" (unanticipated)** | Requires expectations to **adjust** → generates a holding-period return that **differs from the expected return** |

**Why this matters**: This distinction explains **counterintuitive market reactions** to economic data releases.

> **Example logic**: Investors judge data releases **relative to their expectations**, not in absolute terms.
> - Prices can **fall** on "good" news if the actual data was **worse than expected** (i.e., "good" but not *good enough*)
> - Prices can **rise** on "bad" news if the actual data was **better than expected** (i.e., "bad" but not *as bad* as feared)

---

### Economic Factors vs. Investor Sentiment — Two Distinct Channels

While this reading's primary focus is **economic factors**, the text also distinguishes them from **investor sentiment** (enthusiasm/despair):

| Driver | Transmission Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Economic factors** | Generally **direct** effects on **cash flows** and/or **discount rates** |
| **Investor sentiment** | Direct effects on **discount rates** (via higher/lower **risk premiums**), and possibly **indirect** effects on future cash flows |

---

### Exam Tips

- **Core testable concept**: "News" is defined strictly as a **surprise** — information that was already expected is **not** news, even if it's objectively "good" or "bad" in absolute terms
- **Classic exam trap**: A question may describe "positive" economic data accompanied by a **price decline** — the correct explanation is that the data came in **below expectations**, not that markets are behaving irrationally
- Link back to 1.02: news affects value through the **same two channels** as any economic factor — cash flows (numerator) and/or discount rate components (denominator)
- **Sentiment vs. economic factors** is a subtle but testable distinction: sentiment operates primarily through the **risk premium** component of the discount rate ($\gamma_i$ from 1.02), while economic factors can hit **either** cash flows or discount rates directly
- Keep the causal chain straight: **Change in expectations → repricing → realized return ≠ expected return** — this is the mechanism connecting news to holding-period returns
