## Summary: 1.21 | How Big is the Equity Risk Premium? (CFA Level II — Economics and Investment Markets)

---

### Learning Outcome
Same LOS as 1.19 — this section provides the **empirical answer** to the equity risk premium's magnitude.

---

### Recap — Why Equities Command a Risk Premium

$$\boxed{\text{Economic downturn} \implies \text{Corporate profits fall (pro-cyclicality)} \implies \text{Equities pay off poorly PRECISELY when investors need offsetting gains most}}$$

**Core conclusion**: Because of this **pro-cyclicality** between economies and aggregate corporate profits, **equities are a poor hedge** against bad consumption outcomes → investors require compensation → a **positive equity risk premium (ERP)**.

---

### The Central Question — How Large Should the ERP Be?

**Key methodological caveat**:
$$\boxed{\text{The equity risk premium CANNOT be quantified ex ante (looking forward)}}$$

**What we CAN do**: Examine the **ex post** (historical, realized) ERP using **very long runs of data**.

---

### Exhibit 25 — Ex Post Real Returns, 1900–2017 (Dimson, Marsh, Staunton 2018)

**Universal finding**: 
$$\boxed{\text{Over this 117-year period, equities OUTPERFORMED government bonds in EVERY developed-economy market examined}}$$

**Range of ex post equity risk premiums across countries**:

$$\boxed{
\begin{array}{l|c}
\text{Country/Region} & \text{Ex Post ERP (annual)} \\
\hline
\text{Switzerland (lowest)} & 2.2\% \\
\text{Australia (highest)} & 5.1\% \\
\text{United States} & 4.5\% \\
\text{Global equity market (vs. bonds)} & 3.2\%
\end{array}
}$$

**US context**: US equities (world's largest equity market) outperformed **US Treasuries** by an average of **4.5% per year** over the 117-year measurement period.

---

### Interpreting the Evidence — From Historical to Forward-Looking

**Important caveat repeated**:
$$\boxed{\text{No guarantee that a premium earned in the PAST will be earned in the FUTURE}}$$

**Practical takeaway from the long-run data**:
$$\boxed{\text{The ex ante (forward-looking) equity risk premium for developed-economy equity markets is LIKELY somewhere between 3\% and 5\% per year}}$$

This range is presented as a **reasonable estimate/anchor**, derived from — but not guaranteed by — the long historical record.

---

### Exam Tips

- **Critical distinction to memorize**: **ex ante** ERP (forward-looking, cannot be directly quantified) vs. **ex post** ERP (historical/realized, measurable from long-run data) — this distinction is a classic, high-probability exam point
- **Key range to memorize**: the reading concludes a **reasonable ex ante ERP estimate for developed markets is 3%–5% per year**, anchored by the historical global figure (~3.2%) and the US figure (~4.5%)
- **Country range (2.2% Switzerland to 5.1% Australia)** shows the ERP is **not uniform globally** — useful if asked to discuss cross-country variation or reasons the premium might differ (e.g., differing growth, volatility, or risk-aversion characteristics per earlier sections)
- **Source citation**: Dimson, Marsh, and Staunton (2018) is the canonical long-run global equity returns study — worth recognizing by name, as it's a well-known reference in practice and likely referenced elsewhere in the curriculum (e.g., Alternative Investments or asset allocation readings)
- **Past performance ≠ future guarantee** is explicitly stated in the text — a good caveat to include in any essay answer that cites historical ERP figures as a *basis* for forward-looking estimates
- **This section is short and empirical** — no new formulas, but it directly **quantifies** the conceptual framework built in 1.19–1.20 (poor consumption hedge → positive premium), converting theory into a **concrete, memorizable number range**
