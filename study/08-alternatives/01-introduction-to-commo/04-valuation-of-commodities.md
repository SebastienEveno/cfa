## Summary: Valuation of Commodities vs. Equities and Bonds (CFA Level II — Alternative Investments)

---

### Fundamental Distinction: Physical vs. Financial Assets

| Feature | Stocks & Bonds | Commodities |
|---------|---------------|-------------|
| **Asset type** | **Financial assets** — claims on economic output of a business/government | **Physical assets** (mostly) |
| **Cash flows** | Generate **periodic income** (dividends, coupons) | **Incur costs** (storage, transportation) — no periodic income |
| **Valuation basis** | **Discounted future cash flows / profitability** | **Discounted forecast of future prices** (supply, demand, volatility) |
| **Primary investment vehicle** | Direct ownership (shares, bonds) | **Derivative contracts** (futures, swaps) — finite lifetime |
| **"Almost always" physical?** | N/A | Note: some newer commodities (electricity, weather) **are not tangible** |

---

### Commodity Valuation Approach

**Not DCF-based** — instead relies on:
1. **Supply and demand** analysis of the physical commodity
2. **Expected price volatility** forecasts
3. **Storage and transportation costs** (affect forward price curve shape)
4. **Technical analysis** (professional judgment, floor trading experience)

**Formal vs. informal valuation spectrum:**
- **Formal end**: Oil company with detailed cost modeling (extraction, refining, transport margins)
- **Informal end**: Floor trader using professional judgment and technical analysis

---

### Storage, Transportation, and the Forward Price Curve

**Owning a commodity incurs costs** (not income):
$$\text{Spot price outlay} + \text{Storage costs} + \text{Transportation costs} = \text{Total cost of physical ownership}$$

**Effect on futures curve shape:**
- **Substantial storage/transport costs** → futures prices **rise with longer expiration dates** (contango)
- **Strong current demand** → spot price can **exceed near-term futures prices** (backwardation)

> The interplay between storage costs and spot demand determines the **shape of the forward curve** and creates **roll return** opportunities/costs for futures investors.

---

### Arbitrage Limitations in Commodities

**Unlike financial assets**, commodity arbitrage is **imperfect** because:
- Some participants **cannot physically deliver or receive** the commodity
- Arbitrageurs without physical market access cannot fully enforce the law of one price
- Result: Spot-futures price relationship is a **range/boundary**, not an exact equality

$$\text{Financial assets: Spot-futures relationship = equality (fully arbitraged)}$$
$$\text{Commodities: Spot-futures relationship = range (bounded, not exact)}$$

---

### Practical Application: Airlines as Commodity Hedgers

**Airlines**: Highly dependent on jet fuel costs; razor-thin margins; intense fare competition.

**Hedging approach:**
- Buy **long energy futures/swap positions** to lock in future fuel costs
- Protects against surprise oil price shocks
- **Price discovery**: Futures markets provide forward fuel price signals → helps airlines price tickets profitably for future travel

**Key insight**: Airline ticket sales = selling a contract at today's price for **future delivery of a service** → creates natural need to hedge the underlying input cost (jet fuel) with futures.

### Example 6 Answer

**Q1 — In contrast to stocks and bonds, commodities:**

→ **C. Commodity investment is primarily via derivatives**

- **A wrong**: Not ALL commodities are physical (electricity, weather are not tangible)
- **B wrong**: Commodities **incur** costs (storage, transport); they don't generate periodic cash flows like dividends or coupons
- **C correct**: The most common commodity investment vehicle is **derivative contracts** (futures, swaps), not direct physical ownership

---

### Example 7 Answer

**Q1 — Key distinction between commodity and financial asset valuation:**

→ **B. Commodity valuation focuses on supply and demand; stock/bond valuation focuses on discounted cash flows**

- **A wrong**: Technical analysis CAN be applied to commodities (floor traders use it routinely)
- **C wrong**: Has the definitions exactly reversed
- **B correct**: Commodities → supply/demand + price volatility forecast; Stocks/bonds → future profitability/cash flow discounting

---

### Exam Tips

- **Commodities ≠ financial assets**: no periodic income, no cash flow claims — pure price appreciation potential
- **Stocks/bonds = DCF-based; Commodities = supply/demand/price forecast**
- **Primary investment vehicle for commodities = derivatives** (futures, swaps) — not direct physical ownership
- **Storage and transport costs are carry costs** → push futures prices above spot (contango) when significant
- **Spot can exceed futures** (backwardation) when near-term demand is very strong
- **Arbitrage is imperfect** for commodities — physical delivery constraints mean no-arbitrage relationships are ranges, not equalities
- **Technical analysis is valid** for commodity valuation — don't fall for the "only fundamental analysis" trap
- **Electricity and weather** are examples of intangible commodities — good exam trap regarding "always physical"
