## Summary: Contango, Backwardation, and the Roll Return — Historical Analysis (CFA Level II — Alternative Investments)

---

### Key Restatement of Core Concepts

**Contango and backwardation** are not just pricing phenomena — they are **accounting mechanisms** that reflect underlying **supply and demand expectations** in the commodity term structure.

**Roll return** captures this relationship quantitatively as futures contracts are rolled forward.

---

### Historical Pattern of Roll Returns (S&P GSCI, 1970–2019)

**Key findings from historical data:**

| Finding | Implication |
|---------|------------|
| Periods of contango/backwardation **do not persist indefinitely** | Cannot assume a commodity stays in one regime |
| Roll return and price return have only **~3% correlation** | Roll return is NOT indicative of price direction |
| Positive price returns can coincide with **either positive or negative** roll returns | Common misconception that backwardation always = positive returns |

**Historical S&P GSCI roll return**: −1.3% per year (average); standard deviation of 4.7% (highly variable).

---

### Why Different Sectors Have Different Roll Returns

| Sector | Why Negative Roll Return | Special Notes |
|--------|--------------------------|--------------|
| **Agriculture, Livestock, Softs** | Stored in silos, feedlots, warehouses → high storage costs → contango dominant | Seasonal harvests add storage burden |
| **Industrial Metals** | Stored in bonded warehouses → high storage costs → contango | Demand driven by economic cycles |
| **Precious Metals** | Gold perpetually stored as **alternative currency** → always high storage → consistently negative roll | No "consumption" to reduce inventory |
| **Energy** | Consumed on **near-real-time basis** → low storage buffer → lower convenience yield historically | BUT post-2010 dynamics changed this |

---

### Post-2010 Energy Market Structural Change

**Before 2010**: Energy (especially oil) had lowest storage buffer → occasional backwardation → some positive roll returns.

**After 2010 — Three key structural shifts:**
1. **US shale oil production**: Massively increased supply → reduced scarcity risk → lower convenience yield → more contango
2. **China as lead oil importer**: Replaced the US → shifted where supply risk is concentrated
3. **OPEC+ supply restrictions** (including Russia): Cartel regained some pricing power, occasionally creating backwardation episodes

**Net result**: Energy's historical opportunity for positive roll return has **diminished significantly post-2010**.

---

### Sector Implications for Portfolio Construction

| Portfolio Tilt | Expected Roll Return Impact |
|---------------|---------------------------|
| **Overweight agriculture, livestock, precious metals, softs** | **Negative roll returns** likely — high storage costs dominate |
| **Overweight energy** (ex-natural gas) | **Possible positive roll** — IF producers successfully withhold supply; less certain post-2010 |
| **Diversified across sectors** | Roll return depends heavily on sector weights |

> **Critical insight**: Roll return is **highly sector-dependent** → sector diversification or concentration has a profound impact on overall portfolio roll return.

---

### Roll Return vs. Price Return — The Hierarchy

| Component | Relative Importance | Key Characteristic |
|-----------|--------------------|--------------------|
| **Price return** | **Dominant** (dominant driver of monthly returns) | High volatility (19.8%); 97% correlation to total return |
| **Roll return** | **Secondary** | Can matter in any single period; modest over time; 3% correlation to price return |
| **Collateral return** | **Important long-run contributor** | Low volatility; historically largest contributor by magnitude when rates are high |

---

### Example 20 Answer

**Q1 — Roll return's contribution to total return:**

→ **B. Can be important in any single period but is relatively modest over multiple periods**

- **A wrong**: Roll return is NOT consistently significant over both single AND multiple periods — it's modest over time
- **C wrong**: Roll return is NOT always close to zero — it varies significantly by commodity and time period (ranges from −39.5% to +31.5% for energy sector)
- **B correct**: In any given month or year, roll return can be meaningful; but historically, its **average annual contribution is small** (−1.3% for S&P GSCI) compared with the magnitude of price return swings

---

### Exam Tips

- **Roll returns are mostly negative** across sectors — only energy has had historical positive potential (and even that has diminished post-2010)
- **Precious metals and softs** have the most negative roll returns — perpetual storage (gold as currency) and expensive commodity storage
- **Agriculture** is also strongly negative (−4.5%) — seasonal storage in silos
- **Roll return ≠ price return predictor**: Only 3% correlation — you cannot use roll return to forecast spot price direction
- **Contango/backwardation regimes are NOT permanent** — markets shift between the two
- **Post-2010 shale revolution** changed energy's roll dynamics — reduced US oil scarcity = reduced convenience yield = more contango
- **Roll return is sector-dependent** → sector allocation is a major driver of overall portfolio roll return
- **For exam calculations**: Roll return = (Near − Far)/Near × % rolled; negative in contango, positive in backwardation
