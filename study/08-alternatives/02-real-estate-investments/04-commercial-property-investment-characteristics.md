## Summary: Commercial Property Investment Characteristics (CFA Level II — Alternative Investments)

---

### Residential Use Properties

**Primary focus for commercial investors**: Multi-family properties (apartments/flats).

**Categories**: Urban vs. suburban; high-rise vs. garden apartments/townhouses; amenity levels.

**Demand drivers**:
- Macroeconomic: Personal income, job growth
- Local conditions: Availability and affordability of **owner-occupied alternatives**
- Tenant protections (rent controls, eviction limits) vary by jurisdiction
- **Shorter lease terms** than commercial properties → more frequent repricing

**Key income measure:**

$$\boxed{GPRI = \text{Market rent} \times \text{Rentable space}}$$

**GPRI vs. Gross Rental Income**:
- **GPRI** = market rent × full occupancy (theoretical maximum)
- **Gross Rental Income** = current rents × rented space (actual rents vs. market)
- Difference = "Loss to Lease" (current leases below market rent)
- Then subtract vacancies and concessions → Net rental income

---

### Pinebranch Estates Example

**Setup**: 240 units × 1,200 sq ft = 288,000 sq ft; market rent = AUD2.00/sq ft/month

**Annual GPRI** = 2.00 × 288,000 × 12 = **AUD6,912,000**

| Item | AUD |
|------|-----|
| GPRI | 6,912,000 |
| − Loss to Lease | (128,200) |
| − Vacancy & Collection | (791,236) |
| − Concessions | (485,124) |
| + Other Income | 295,211 |
| + Expense Recovery | 525,800 |
| **Effective Gross Income** | **6,328,451** |
| − Operating & Leasing Expenses | (2,753,000) |
| − Property Maintenance Allowance | (655,000) |
| **NOI** | **2,920,451** |

**Pinebranch vs. Wallonia contrast**:
- **Pinebranch**: Multiple tenants; lease rollovers frequent; vacancy and concession risk high; GPRI-based analysis; market rent changes matter
- **Wallonia**: Single tenant; fully leased for 5 years; fixed income stream; limited vacancy risk; operating expense recovery structure

### Commercial Use Properties — Sector Overview

#### Office Properties

**Demand driver**: Historically service industry employment; post-COVID shift to **remote/hybrid work** has structurally reduced demand.

**Types**: Multi-tenant CBD towers → Single-tenant suburban offices → Purpose-built (medical, corporate HQ).

**Development approach**: Often begins when an **anchor tenant** commits to a large portion of space → reduces development risk.

**Key risk**: Structural shift away from in-person work (secular, not cyclical).

---

#### Industrial and Warehouse Properties

**Demand driver**: Distribution, logistics, manufacturing needs; **e-commerce boom** caused structural increase in warehouse demand.

**Key features**:
- Often **purpose-built for specific use** → **most difficult to convert** to alternative uses
- Those in desirable areas more often adapted

**Post-COVID impact**: **Positive** — rents rose at 2–3× pre-pandemic growth rates as supply chains regionalized.

---

#### Retail Properties

**Types**: Regional malls/shopping centers (anchor stores + in-line) → Local centers → Standalone stores (grocery, restaurants).

**Key risk**: **Structural shift to e-commerce** has reduced shopping center/mall demand → many converted to alternative uses.

**Location factors**: Proximity to local workers and residents drives rents, occupancy, and values.

**Often combined**: With office or residential space (lower floors in CBDs).

---

#### Hospitality Properties

**Demand varies widely by purpose and clientele:**

| Type | Business Drivers | Cycle Sensitivity |
|------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Business travel hotels** (airport, CBD) | Corporate travel budgets | **Highly cyclical** — firms cut in downturns |
| **Convention centers** | Major trade shows (multiyear contracts) | Cyclical but with longer visibility |
| **Economy motels/hotels** | Households and workers in transit | **Less cyclical** |
| **Tourist/vacation hotels** | Consumer confidence, disposable income | Moderate cyclicality |
| **Destination resorts** | Premium discretionary spending | **Most management-intensive**; high fixed costs |

---

#### Specialty Properties

| Type | Key Feature |
|------|------------|
| **Self-storage** | Low capital requirements; low operating costs; month-to-month leases → pricing flexibility but higher demand volatility |
| **Senior housing** | Range from independent living (low complexity) to continuing care (high complexity); aging demographics support demand in developed markets |
| **Cell towers, data centers, parking** | Infrastructure-like characteristics; often long-term contracts |
| **Hospitals/medical facilities** | Defensive; stable demand regardless of cycle |

---

### Mixed-Use Development

**Definition**: Combines multiple tenant types and economic uses (e.g., office + retail + hospitality).

**Benefits**: Diversified income streams across different activity types.

**Key risk**: Despite diversification, income streams are **often correlated** because tenants serve each other's customers (e.g., retail customers = office workers in the same building).

**Worst-case scenario for mixed-use**: **National economic recession** — affects ALL tenant types simultaneously.

---

### Eastmain Plaza Example (Mixed-Use)

**Setup**: 350,000 sq ft; 90% office at MYR100/ft²; 10% retail at MYR250/ft²

**Q1 — Gross Rent Calculation:**
- Office: 350,000 × 90% × 100 = MYR31,500,000
- Retail: 350,000 × 10% × 250 = MYR8,750,000
- **Total gross rent = MYR40,250,000** ✓ (matches income statement)

**Q2 — Downside: Retail rent falls 50%:**
- Retail rent reduction = MYR8,750,000 × 50% = MYR4,375,000
- But retail is fully leased → vacancy/concession lines unchanged (both zero for retail)
- **NOI decreases by MYR4,375,000** (all other expenses and income unchanged)
- New NOI = 13,085,217 − 4,375,000 = **MYR8,710,217**

---

### Question Set Answers

**Q1** (GPRI vs. Gross Rental Income):
→ **B. The difference reflects the gap between market rents and current rents (Loss to Lease)**
- A wrong: Vacancy is deducted from **gross rental income** to get net rental income — it's not the GPRI vs. GRI gap
- C wrong: Concessions are also deducted from gross rental income, not GPRI vs. GRI

**Q2** (Which property type is LEAST likely to be converted due to structural change?):
→ **C. Industrial and warehouse properties** — purpose-built for specific uses; physically difficult to repurpose
- A wrong: **Retail** heavily impacted by e-commerce → many conversions happening now
- B wrong: **Office** impacted by remote work → significant conversion activity post-COVID

**Q3** (Worst-case for mixed-use office/retail/business hotel):
→ **C. National economic recession affecting business travel**
- Affects ALL three property types: hotel occupancy falls, retail suffers (fewer guests/workers), office rents/vacancies worsen
- A wrong: Only office tenant affected; some ripple effect on retail but limited
- B wrong: Primarily affects retail foot traffic and hotel entrance; temporary; doesn't affect office

### Key Comparative Table

| Property Type | Demand Driver | Key Risk | COVID Impact | Cyclicality |
|--------------|--------------|---------|-------------|-------------|
| **Multi-family residential** | Income, affordability of ownership | Rent controls, tenant turnover | Mixed (↑ suburban, ↓ urban) | Moderate |
| **Office** | Service employment | Remote/hybrid work shift | **Negative** (structural) | Moderate-High |
| **Industrial/Warehouse** | Distribution, e-commerce | Specific-use inflexibility | **Positive** (structural) | Low-Moderate |
| **Retail** | Consumer spending | E-commerce shift | **Negative** (structural) | Moderate |

| **Self-storage** | Broad household/business use | Month-to-month demand volatility | Positive | Low |

### Exam Tips

- **GPRI = market rent × rentable space** (assumes full occupancy at market rates)
- **Loss to Lease** = GPRI − Gross Rental Income (current rents below market → opportunity as leases roll)
- **Industrial/warehouse**: Least adaptable to alternative uses (purpose-built) AND benefited from e-commerce surge
- **Office**: Structural headwind from remote/hybrid work (secular change, not just cyclical)
- **Retail**: Structural headwind from e-commerce (many converting to alternative uses)
- **Business hotels**: Most cyclical hospitality type; destination resorts are management-intensive with high fixed costs
- **Self-storage**: Month-to-month leases → pricing flexibility but demand sensitivity
- **Mixed-use correlation risk**: Revenue streams appear diversified but tenants often serve each other → correlated in downturns
- **Worst-case for mixed-use** = broad economic recession (hits ALL tenant types simultaneously)
- **Anchor tenant**: Commits to large portion of space → reduces new development risk (especially for office)
