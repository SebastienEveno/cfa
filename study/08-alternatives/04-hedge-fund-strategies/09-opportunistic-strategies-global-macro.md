## Summary: 4.09 | Opportunistic Strategies: Global Macro Strategies (CFA Level II — Alternative Investments)

---

### Learning Outcome
Discuss investment characteristics, strategy implementation, and role in a portfolio of **opportunistic** hedge fund strategies.

*(New category — transitions from relative value to opportunistic strategies.)*

---

### Opportunistic Strategies — Overview

$$\boxed{\text{Opportunistic strategies: profit across WIDE ranges of markets/securities using diverse techniques — TOP-DOWN focus on asset classes, sectors, regions, macro themes, multi-asset relationships GLOBALLY (not individual security level)}}$$

**Three classification dimensions**:
1. **Analytical approach**: technical vs. **fundamental**
2. **Implementation**: **discretionary** vs. **systematic**
3. **Instruments/markets traded**

**Fundamental-based**: uses economic data → fair valuation of securities/sectors/markets/intra-market relationships
**Technical**: statistical methods predicting price movements from past trends

**Discretionary**: relies on manager skill/judgment; subject to **behavioral biases** (overconfidence, loss aversion)
**Systematic**: rules-based, computer-algorithm-executed; struggles with **novel, unprecedented situations**

**Systematic "herding" risk**:
$$\boxed{\text{Growing size of systematic trend-following funds} \implies \text{Simultaneous model reversals can create a "HERDING EFFECT"} \implies \text{Temporarily overwhelms liquidity, distorts fundamental pricing (trend "overshoots")}}$$

**Two most common opportunistic strategies**: **global macro** and **managed futures** (managed futures covered in the next section).

---

## Global Macro Strategies

$$\boxed{\text{Global macro: trades GLOBAL relationships across wide asset classes/instruments — derivatives on commodities, currencies, metals, FI/equity indexes, plus sovereign/corporate bonds and individual stocks}}$$

**Common themes/approaches**:
- **Themes**: e.g., undervalued EM currencies vs. overvalued USD (via OTC currency swaps)
- **Regions**: e.g., Italy FTSE MIB vs. Germany DAX futures (eurozone valuation gaps)
- **Styles**: systematic vs. discretionary spread trading (e.g., energy futures)

**Focus areas for manager views**: relative economic health, central bank policy, global yield curve relationships, inflation/purchasing power parity trends, capital/trade flows (often expressed via currency or rate-curve positioning).

---

### Anticipatory and Contrarian Positioning

$$\boxed{\text{Global macro managers tend to be ANTICIPATORY and sometimes CONTRARIAN — often EARLY in positioning, profiting as rationality eventually returns}}$$

**Case study — 2006–2008 subprime crisis**:
$$\boxed{\text{Managers sensed the subprime crisis as early as 2006} \implies \text{Bought CDS protection on mortgage bonds/tranches/credit indexes}}$$
- Had to **wait until 2007–2008** for payoff, but performed **spectacularly** once the GFC materialized
- Illustrates the strategy's value as a **portfolio hedge against sudden market reversals**

**Heterogeneity caveat**:
$$\boxed{\text{Global macro managers are HETEROGENEOUS as a group} \implies \text{Less consistently dependable as a "short alpha" source than pure systematic trend-following managed futures}}$$
- **But**: their **anticipatory** nature (vs. managed futures' typically reactive/trend-following nature) is a valuable distinguishing attribute

---

### Investment Characteristics

**Views can be**: 
$$\boxed{\text{DIRECTIONAL (e.g., buy bank bonds benefiting from rate normalization)} \quad \text{OR THEMATIC (e.g., long Brexit "winners," short "losers")}}$$

**Due diligence implication**: given heterogeneity, allocators need **close attention to current portfolio positioning** to correctly anticipate a given manager's factor risk exposures.

### Leverage

$$\boxed{\text{Margin-to-equity ratio typically 15\%–25\% on futures/forwards} \implies \text{Controls face amounts up to 6x to 7x fund assets}}$$

### Return Drivers and Risk Environment

$$\boxed{\text{Key return source: correctly identifying/capitalizing on GLOBAL MARKET TRENDS}}$$

$$\boxed{\text{Natural ENEMY: mean-reverting, LOW volatility markets}}$$

**Opportunity-rich environments** (also risk sources): steep equity sell-offs, interest rate regime changes, currency devaluations, volatility spikes, geopolitical shocks (trade wars, terrorism).

**Return character**: 
$$\boxed{\text{LUMPIER, UNEVEN return streams, generally HIGHER volatility than other hedge fund strategies}}$$
- Risk: selected exposures may **not react as expected** if global risks don't materialize or unforeseen contrary factors emerge

### The Post-GFC QE Challenge

$$\boxed{\text{Post-2007-09 QE era} \implies \text{Benign markets, LOW volatility across equities, fixed income, currencies, commodities} \implies \text{DIFFICULT environment for global macro}}$$

**Central bank intervention effect**: dampened trendiness/volatility → **the lifeblood of global macro strategies** → caused some allocators to **avoid** the strategy during this period.

**Reading's caution**: this avoidance **may be shortsighted** — opportunistic strategies like global macro remain valuable for **diversification and alpha over a FULL market cycle**. 

**Recent validation**: **COVID-19 pandemic** and the resulting **sharp monetary policy reversal** demonstrated renewed opportunities for global macro strategies.

---

### Strategy Implementation

$$\boxed{\text{Global macro: TOP-DOWN approach, uses macroeconomic/fundamental models to express DIRECTIONAL or RELATIVE VALUE views}}$$

**Instrument mix**: individual securities, security baskets, index futures, FX futures/forwards, metals futures, agricultural futures, fixed-income products/futures, and derivatives/options on any of these.

**Directional vs. relative value distinction**:
- **Directional**: fundamental data determines if a market/asset is under/overvalued vs. history and expected macro trend
- **Relative value**: which assets are under/overvalued **relative to EACH OTHER** given macro conditions

**Worked illustration — ASEAN currency example**: 
- ASEAN currencies depreciating vs. USD → **directional model**: buy shares of key exporters (undervalued from currency weakness)
- **Deeper analysis** might reveal: exporter **bonds** are cheap relative to their **shares** (because shares react to currency moves faster than bonds) → **relative value trade**: buy bonds, short shares

### Success Requirements and Portfolio Construction Caveats

$$\boxed{\text{Success requires: (1) CORRECT fundamental view AND (2) PROPER methodology/timing for tactical implementation}}$$

**Risk of poor timing**: managers implementing positions **too early**, unwinding **too late**, or using **inappropriate implementation methods** → face **investor redemptions**.

**Diversification caveat — a key nuance**:
$$\boxed{\text{Despite holding MANY simultaneous positions (enabled by leverage), diversification benefits are LESS than in idiosyncratic L/S equity strategies}}$$
- **Why**: "risk-on/risk-off" market conditions (often central-bank-driven) tend to move **multiple asset classes in a CORRELATED manner** — so nominal position count doesn't equal true diversification

### Worked Example 8 — EM Currency Crisis Anticipation

**Scenario**: EM countries overheating — large budget/trade deficits, ongoing central bank currency intervention, rising political support for high taxes/welfare spending. Manager Melvin Chu identifies a leading EM country's central bank is **running low on FX reserves** → may soon be **unable to continue currency support**.

**Q: How might Chu position using options to profit from these trends?**

**Expected contagion logic**: 
$$\boxed{\text{Initial devaluation} \implies \text{Likely triggers a WAVE of devaluations/turbulence across similarly-positioned EM countries}}$$

**Recommended trades** — target: EM currency depreciation, rising EM rates, falling EM equities, EM volatility spikes:

$$\boxed{\text{Use OPTIONS (specifically PUTS) to CAP downside risk at premium paid, rather than direct short positions}}$$

**Specific structure**:
1. **Buy puts** on: various EM currencies, EM government bond futures, EM equity indexes
2. **Sizing by conviction**: 
$$\boxed{\text{High-conviction trades} \to \text{ITM puts} \qquad \text{Lower-conviction trades} \to \text{OTM puts}}$$
3. **Flight-to-safety hedge**: buy **call options** on DM reserve currencies AND call options on **highly-rated DM government bond futures**

**Key risk management principle**: 
$$\boxed{\text{If the thesis fails to materialize, losses are CAPPED at total premiums paid} — \text{options provide defined, limited downside vs. unlimited-loss direct positioning}}$$

### Exam Tips

- **Three classification dimensions** (fundamental/technical, discretionary/systematic, instrument type) are a good organizing framework — expect a question asking you to classify a described strategy along these axes
- **Herding/systematic trend-following overshoot risk** is a distinctive, testable concept — know that GROWING AUM in systematic strategies can itself create **market distortion** via simultaneous position reversals
- **2006–2008 subprime CDS case study** is an excellent, citable real-world illustration of the "anticipatory, early-positioning" character of global macro — good for essay questions on strategy timing/patience requirements
- **Leverage figures (15%–25% margin-to-equity → 6x–7x notional exposure)** are specific and testable
- **The "natural enemy" concept — low-volatility, mean-reverting markets — is central**: this directly explains why the **post-GFC QE decade was a difficult environment** for global macro, a good cause-effect exam link
- **Diversification caveat is a subtle but important, testable nuance**: many simultaneous global macro positions ≠ true diversification, because risk-on/risk-off regimes create **correlated** moves across asset classes — don't assume position count alone reduces portfolio risk
- **ASEAN currency example** is a clean template for distinguishing **directional** vs. **relative value** implementation of the **same underlying macro thesis** — a good exam pattern to recognize (bonds lag shares in reacting to currency moves → relative value opportunity)
- **Example 8's options-based approach** teaches a key risk management principle: using **long options (puts/calls)** rather than direct short/long positions **caps downside at the premium** — a good template for "how would you express this macro view with limited downside" questions
- **ITM vs. OTM put sizing by conviction level** is a specific, testable implementation detail worth remembering
- This section begins the **opportunistic category** — expect the **next section** to cover **managed futures**, the other named opportunistic strategy, completing this category before the reading moves to **specialist strategies** (volatility, reinsurance)
