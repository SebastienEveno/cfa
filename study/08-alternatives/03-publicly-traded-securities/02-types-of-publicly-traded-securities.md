## Summary: Types of Publicly Traded Real Estate Securities (CFA Level II — Alternative Investments)

---

### Overview — Three Principal Types

| Security Type | What It Represents |
|---------------|---------------------|
| **REITs** | Equity ownership in income-producing real estate |
| **REOCs** | Ordinary taxable real estate companies |
| **MBS** | Securitized debt backed by mortgage loan pools |

**Securitization's role**: Makes real estate accessible to investors of all sizes — previously only available to the largest institutional investors.

---

### REITs — Structure and Types

**REIT**: Company that owns, finances, and (to a limited extent) develops income-producing real estate.

$$\boxed{\text{REITs distribute } 90\%\text{–}100\% \text{ of taxable income to shareholders}}$$

| Type | Activity |
|------|---------|
| **Equity REITs** | Own real estate directly |
| **Mortgage REITs** | Make or invest in loans secured by real estate |

**Tax advantage mechanism**: REITs deduct dividends paid from taxable income → effectively **exempt from corporate income tax** in many jurisdictions → avoids double taxation (income taxed only once, at the investor level).

---

### REOCs — When Companies Use This Structure Instead

A real estate company is organized as a **REOC** (not a REIT) when:

1. **Located in a country without a tax-advantaged REIT regime**
2. **Engages significantly in development of for-sale properties** (not just income-producing rentals)
3. **Offers non-qualifying services** — e.g., brokerage, third-party property management

> REOCs are **ordinary taxable corporations** — no special pass-through tax treatment.

---

### Mortgage-Backed Securities (MBS)

**Definition**: Securitized debt obligations representing rights to cash flows from **pools of mortgage loans**.

| Type | Underlying | Typical Pool Size |
|------|-----------|-------------------|
| **RMBS** (Residential) | Residential mortgages | **Thousands** of loans |
| **CMBS** (Commercial) | Commercial mortgages | **~100 loans** down to **as few as 1** (for very large assets) |

**Key market fact**: The market value of real estate **debt securities** (especially RMBS) **greatly exceeds** the market cap of real estate **equity** securities (REITs/REOCs).

---

### Private Real Estate Securities (Alternative to Public)

Beyond publicly traded securities, private forms include:
- Private REITs
- Private REOCs
- Privately held mortgages
- Private debt issues
- Bank debt

> Many **real estate private equity partnerships** create **private REITs** specifically to hold income-producing properties.

---

### REIT Qualification Requirements (Most Countries)

| Requirement | Typical Threshold |
|-------------|-------------------|
| **Distribution** | 90%–100% of taxable earnings |
| **Asset composition** | ≥75% of assets in real estate |
| **Income composition** | ≥75% from rental income or mortgage interest |

**Additional country-specific restrictions may include:**
- Minimum number of shareholders
- Maximum ownership concentration by a single shareholder
- Minimum property count / maximum asset concentration
- Maximum non-rental income
- Maximum development activity
- Leverage and loan-type limits

**US-specific rule — "5/50 Rule":**
$$\boxed{\text{Minimum 100 shareholders; fewer than 5 shareholders cannot own} > 50\% \text{ of shares}}$$

> **Purpose**: Prevents small groups from creating REITs to hold individual assets for personal tax benefit — ensures REITs remain broadly held investment vehicles.

---

### REIT Management Structures

| Structure | Description | Conflict Level |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Self-managed/self-advised** | Executives are company employees reporting to board/trustees (elected by shareholders) | **Lower** — most US REITs use this model |
| **Externally managed/advised** | Third-party adviser paid asset management fees | **Higher** — potential conflicts |

**Key conflict with external management**:
- Fees often based on **total assets** → adviser has an incentive to **grow the REIT** even if not value-accretive
- External managers may also require the REIT to pay **affiliated service providers** for property management, acquisitions, debt placement — additional fee layers

### Advantages of REITs (vs. Private Real Estate)

| Advantage | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Liquidity** | Buy/sell shares in almost any amount on major exchanges |
| **Transparency** | Readily available share prices and transaction histories |
| **Diversification** | Across property types, geography, tenant credit quality |
| **High-quality portfolios** | Many REITs own premium assets in leading markets |
| **Professional management** | Dedicated teams with economies of scale |
| **Potentially stable income** | Well-leased properties → predictable income (some pay monthly distributions) |
| **Tax efficiency** | Single taxation (avoid corporate tax; only investor pays on dividends) |

---

### Disadvantages of REITs

| Disadvantage | Detail |
|--------------|--------|
| **Lack of retained earnings** | Must distribute 90%+ → must access capital markets for growth funding; faster growth = more frequent capital raises |
| **Regulatory costs** | Public company compliance burden (filings, governance) |
| **Reduced diversification benefit** | REIT share prices influenced by **stock market movements**, not purely underlying real estate value → less diversification benefit vs. private real estate |
| **Limited asset types** | Restricted to REIT-qualifying activities → often creates **Taxable REIT Subsidiaries (TRS)** for non-qualifying activities (merchant development, third-party property management) that pay regular corporate tax |

---

### Taxable REIT Subsidiaries (TRS)

**Purpose**: Allows REITs to engage in **non-qualifying activities** without jeopardizing REIT tax status.

**How it works**:
- Activities like development-for-sale or third-party management are conducted through a **separate taxable subsidiary**
- TRS pays regular corporate income tax on its earnings
- Parent REIT retains its tax-advantaged status for qualifying rental income

---

### Exam Tips

- **REITs = equity or mortgage focus**; must distribute 90–100% of taxable income
- **REOCs = taxable corporations**; used when no REIT tax regime exists, heavy development activity, or non-qualifying services
- **RMBS pools = thousands of loans**; **CMBS pools = ~100 or fewer** (sometimes single-asset)
- **Real estate debt market >> equity market** in size (RMBS particularly large)
- **US "5/50 rule"**: 100+ shareholders minimum; top 5 can't own >50% — prevents concentrated private ownership disguised as a REIT
- **Self-managed REITs generally have fewer conflicts** than externally managed REITs (fee-based incentive misalignment)
- **North America = REIT-dominant (98%)**; **Europe/Asia Pacific = more REOC usage** — driven by tax regime differences
- **TRS**: allows REITs to conduct non-qualifying activities in a separate taxable entity without losing REIT status
- **REIT disadvantage**: must constantly access capital markets since retained earnings are minimal — growth-dependent on external financing
- **Public REIT pricing** reflects both real estate fundamentals AND broader stock market sentiment — reduces diversification benefit vs. private real estate
